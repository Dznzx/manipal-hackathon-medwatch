# MedWatch — 2-Minute Team Demo Script

Live URL: https://manipal-hackathon-medwatch.vercel.app
One person drives the screen (whoever's most comfortable clicking live) while the others narrate their part — practice the handoffs twice so nobody talks over a click.

**Speaking time:** Priyal and Tanvi carry the core demo (~65s combined — the two most important beats: the core insight, and redistribution + AI). Allwin and Dinesh bookend it with the setup and the close (~25s combined). Total: 2:00.

Reset the simulation (top-right button) before recording so you start at Day 0.

---

## 0:00–0:12 — Dinesh (opening hook)

Screen: full dashboard, Day 0.

> "A health facility running low on one medicine looks like routine noise — until nearby facilities start running low on the *same* one, at the *same* time. That's MedWatch: it catches that pattern early."

---

## 0:12–0:25 — Allwin (what it is, one line)

Screen: stays on the dashboard; maybe a slow pan/no click yet.

> "It's a real-time dashboard built on real government health facilities in Udupi district, Karnataka — forecasting shortages, telling isolated problems apart from regional ones, and recommending where surplus stock should go."

---

## 0:25–1:05 — Priyal (the core insight — the most important 40 seconds)

Click into a facility detail drawer briefly, then point at the Regional Risk Alerts panel.

> "Here's the core idea. Every facility forecasts its own stockout date with a confidence score — never pretending to be more certain than the data supports. Now watch this: three real facilities in Kundapura Taluk are all trending toward an Amoxicillin stockout within days of each other. MedWatch flags that as a **Regional Risk** — not a guess, a pattern.
>
> Compare that to Udupi Taluk, where one facility alone is low on Insulin — nothing nearby confirms it, so MedWatch calls that an **Isolated Event**. It even reads *why* the regional pattern is happening — demand spike, or a supply-chain delay — because that changes what a health official should actually do about it."

Point at the Projected Risk Trend chart.

> "And this curve shows exactly how that local signal could grow into a wider disruption over the next 20 days if nobody acts."

---

## 1:05–1:45 — Tanvi (redistribution + AI — the second most important 40 seconds)

Point at the Redistribution Suggestions panel and the map's arrows.

> "Once a shortage is flagged, MedWatch finds the nearest facility with real surplus and recommends a transfer — real GPS distance, real impact: 'without this, stockout in 11 days; with it, pushed to 18.' Nothing here is a black box.
>
> And there's a real AI layer on top of all of this — Ask MedWatch. You can type a question in plain English, like 'which facility needs urgent action right now,' and it answers using only the numbers the engine already computed. It can't invent a fact that isn't there. So you get the speed of AI without losing the trust of a transparent system underneath it."

Click one suggested question in the Ask MedWatch panel if time allows; otherwise just point at it.

---

## 1:45–2:00 — Dinesh + Allwin (close, split the last line)

**Dinesh:** "Real facilities, real geography, a transparent engine —"
**Allwin:** "— and AI that explains, not replaces. That's MedWatch."

End on the full dashboard, Day 0.

---

## Notes for whoever's driving the screen

- Keep the slider moves (if any) in the Day 0–10 range — past ~day 15 extra noise appears from simulated data, not worth it in a 2-minute cut.
- If a "Why?" popover doesn't close between clicks, click any blank space first.
- Full-screen the browser (hide the URL bar / bookmarks) before recording.
- Rehearse the two speaker handoffs (Allwin→Priyal, Tanvi→the split close) out loud once — those are the only places timing can slip.
