# How to record the demo video (for whoever's doing it)

Live app: **https://manipal-hackathon-medwatch.vercel.app**
Full script to read/follow: see `VIDEO_SCRIPT.md` in this folder.

## 1. Record the screen (Mac, no install needed)

1. Press **Cmd + Shift + 5**. A small toolbar appears at the bottom of the screen.
2. Click **"Record Selected Portion"** (the dashed-rectangle icon), then drag a
   box around just the browser window — cleaner than recording the whole screen.
3. Click **Options** in that toolbar → set the microphone to your Mac's built-in
   mic (or headset mic) if you're narrating live. Also turn on "Show Mouse
   Clicks" so viewers can see what you're clicking.
4. Open **https://manipal-hackathon-medwatch.vercel.app** in your browser,
   full-screen the browser window (hide bookmarks bar for a clean look), and
   click **Reset simulation** so you start at Day 0.
5. Click **Record** in the toolbar. A countdown plays, then it starts.
6. Follow `VIDEO_SCRIPT.md` step by step — it tells you exactly what to click
   and what to say, timed to fit 3 minutes.
7. Press **Cmd + Shift + 5** again and click **Stop** (or click the stop icon
   in the menu bar) when you're done.
8. The recording saves to your Desktop as a `.mov` file automatically.

## 2. If you mess up a section

Don't restart from scratch — just re-record that section separately and trim/
combine in **iMovie** (also free, built into every Mac):
1. Open iMovie → New Project → Movie.
2. Drag your `.mov` clip(s) onto the timeline in order.
3. Trim a clip by dragging its edges, or split it (click the clip, press
   Cmd+B) to cut out a bad section.
4. File → Share → File... to export as an `.mp4` (choose 1080p, "High" quality).

## 3. Narration tips

- Read the script's quoted lines naturally, don't rush — 3 minutes is not
  much time but the script is already sized for it.
- If recording without live narration, record the screen actions silently
  first, then in iMovie use **Voiceover** (the microphone icon in the toolbar)
  to record narration synced to what's on screen.

## 4. Exporting the final file

- iMovie export settings: **1080p**, **MP4**, "High" quality is plenty —
  don't go higher, hackathon portals usually cap upload size (commonly
  100MB–500MB; check Manipal's Round 1 submission page for the actual limit).
- If the exported file is too large, drop the resolution to 720p in the same
  export dialog — still looks sharp for a dashboard screen-recording.

## 5. Submitting

- Check the Manipal Hackathon 2026 Round 1 submission portal/form for exactly
  how they want the video delivered — the two common patterns are:
  - **Direct upload**: attach the `.mp4` file to the submission form.
  - **Link submission**: upload the video privately/unlisted to YouTube or
    Google Drive first, then paste the link into the form.
- If it's a link submission and you use YouTube: Upload → set visibility to
  **Unlisted** (not Public, not Private) so judges can open the link without
  needing your account, but it won't show up in search.
- Also include the live app link in your submission notes if there's a field
  for it: **https://manipal-hackathon-medwatch.vercel.app** — letting judges
  click around themselves (beyond the video) can only help.
- Double-check the deadline and file-naming convention the portal asks for
  before you submit — this part varies by hackathon and I don't have Manipal's
  exact portal in front of me, so confirm it directly from their instructions.
